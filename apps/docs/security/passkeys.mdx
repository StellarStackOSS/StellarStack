Passkeys are the modern alternative to passwords + TOTP. They're
WebAuthn under the hood — a public-key pair generated on your device
(or hardware key) and bound to the panel's domain. The private key
never leaves your device.

Practical effect: signing in is "tap your phone / press your YubiKey",
no codes to type, and phishing pages can't steal your credential.

## Enrolling a passkey

**Account → Security → Add passkey**.

Your browser prompts for the platform authenticator. Pick:

- **This device** — Touch ID / Windows Hello / Android biometrics.
- **A USB security key** — YubiKey, Titan, etc.
- **A different device** — your phone via QR-based cross-device auth.

Multiple passkeys per account is encouraged. Add one for your laptop,
one for your phone, one as a hardware-key backup.

## Signing in

On the sign-in page, click **Sign in with passkey**. Your browser
prompts for any passkey bound to the panel's domain. Pick one,
authenticate, you're in.

If you've enrolled passkeys, the panel:

- Doesn't require a password.
- Doesn't require TOTP.
- Doesn't allow magic-link or email recovery into the same session.

Passwords stay enabled as a fallback — you can disable them per-user
in **Account → Security → Sign-in methods**.

## What passkeys protect against

- **Phishing.** A fake panel page can't ask your authenticator for a
  signature bound to its domain — the cryptography refuses.
- **Credential reuse.** Passkeys are per-site by construction.
- **Replay.** Each authentication is a fresh challenge-response.

## What passkeys *don't* protect against

- Malware on the device that bypasses the authenticator API.
- Physical compromise of an unlocked device — the same threat model as
  any logged-in browser session.

For very high-value admin accounts on shared machines, pair a hardware
passkey (YubiKey) with a session timeout setting on **Admin → Settings**.

## Recovery

If you lose every device with a passkey enrolled:

- If you also have password + TOTP enabled, sign in via that path and
  enrol new passkeys.
- If you have *only* passkeys and they're all lost, an admin must
  reset the account and you re-enrol.

Same as TOTP, there's no email-link recovery. That's the point.

## Browser support

Passkeys work in:

- Safari 16+ (macOS Ventura, iOS 16).
- Chrome 109+ (desktop + Android).
- Firefox 122+ (desktop; sync requires Firefox account).
- Edge 109+.

Older browsers fall back to password + TOTP.

## Multi-device sync

Apple, Google, and Microsoft sync passkeys across your devices via iCloud
Keychain, Google Password Manager, and Microsoft account respectively.
1Password and Bitwarden also store passkeys.

You don't need to enrol the same passkey on every device — synced
passkeys appear automatically once you sign into the host's password
manager.
